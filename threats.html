Common threats
These are the most common ways attackers try to get into accounts. Click a card to see how to stay safe.
Phishing
Fake emails, messages or websites that pretend to be a real company so you give away your password.
Show how to stay safe
Do not click links from people you do not know. Check the sender address. Type the website address yourself.
Brute force attack
A program tries thousands of passwords every second until one of them works.
Show how to stay safe
Use long passwords and turn on two-factor authentication. Many sites also lock the account after many wrong tries.
Credential stuffing
Attackers take a leaked password from one site and try it on many other sites.
Show how to stay safe
Use a different password for every account. A password manager makes this easy.
Malware
Harmful software such as viruses and spyware. It can steal your data or watch what you type.
Show how to stay safe
Install apps only from trusted stores. Keep your device and antivirus updated.
Social engineering
Attackers trick people instead of computers. For example, someone pretends to be IT support.
Show how to stay safe
Never share your password or OTP code with anyone. If unsure, call the official phone number.
Ransomware
Software that locks your files and asks for money to unlock them.
Show how to stay safe
Keep backups of important files. Do not open unknown attachments. Install updates.
Public Wi-Fi spying
Someone on the same network can watch the data that you send.
Show how to stay safe
Avoid banking on public Wi-Fi. Use websites that start with https. A VPN can help.
Shoulder surfing
Someone watches you type your password or PIN from behind or from far away.
Show how to stay safe
Cover the keypad, use a screen lock and be careful in public places.
